 Business Case Summary 
The CSIS environment's current IT support procedure is a manual, email-based model that is inefficient, unstructured, and untraceable. Missed requests, fragmented communication, and an inability to gauge performance are the results of this. To centralize all IT requests, this project suggests creating a unique Integrated IT Ticketing and Log System. Structured ticket logging, real-time ticket-based communication, job cards with time tracking, and reporting are among the key features.
Feasibility Study Summary 
A Commercial Off-The-Shelf (COTS) system, an Open-Source ticketing system, and a custom web-based application were the three possible solutions that were assessed. With a score of 4 out of 10, the Custom Web-Based Application (MERN Stack) came in first. It removes the technical limitations of altering pre-built open-source platforms, uses internal student developers to guarantee zero licensing costs, and offers high customization for workflows (like job card time tracking and mobile accessibility).
Functional Specification Summary
The system will be a web-based, responsive application that satisfies WCAG 2.1 accessibility requirements. A Technician Dashboard with start/stop "job card" timers, a Student/Staff Portal for ticket logging, and an Admin and Reporting Dashboard for monitoring workload trends and turnaround times are among the key features.
Technical Specification Summary 
The application will be developed using the MERN stack (MongoDB, Express.js, React.js, Node.js). The system will integrate directly with the NWU SMTP Mail Relay for automated notifications, and it will rely on localized MongoDB hosting to guarantee POPIA compliance.
Assumptions: The NWU email infrastructure is dependable, and student developers have regular weekly hours to devote.
Constraints: The project must align with the 2026 academic calendar and university recess periods while remaining within a strict zero-budget limit using internal university resources only.

Scope Management
Delivering a web-based portal for staff and students, a technician dashboard with start/stop time tracking, and automated SMTP email notifications are all included in the project scope.
Scope Management Strategy 
Scope creep will be specifically monitored, and daily tasks and feature requests will be tracked using a Sprint Backlog and Kanban Board. Any feature request that deviates from the baseline specifications will be routinely marked and rejected if it calls for paid APIs.

Acceptance Process 
Agile sprints are in line with the iterative approach used in the acceptance process. Deliverables are submitted to the project sponsor (CSIS IT Supervisor) for official approval after being internally validated by the development team through peer reviews and unit testing. A systematic User Acceptance Testing (UAT) phase is necessary for final system acceptance. 
Activities
Peer Code Reviews: Internal review of codebase against performance and security standards.
Sprint Demos: Bi-weekly live demonstrations of completed system features.
User Acceptance Testing (UAT): Providing a controlled sandbox environment for real users.
Defect Triage: Logging and resolving any bugs identified before final sign-off.

Summary
Early peer review and a final official UAT sandbox session are crucial for acceptance. This ensures that, before deployment, all workflows, from tracking job card time to logging tickets, meet end-user requirements and quality standards.

QUALITY PLAN
Quality Targets
Structured Ticket Logging: Users must be able to log a ticket in under 60 seconds (WCAG 2.1 standards).
Technician Time Tracking: System calculations must be 100% accurate, capturing timestamps down to the second in the MongoDB database.
Automated Communications: 100% delivery success rate for automated emails within 2 minutes of a status trigger.
Secure Data Handling: Strict adherence to POPIA with zero critical vulnerabilities during security audits.

CONCLUSION
The CSIS IT Ticket and Log System is designed to solve the critical inefficiencies caused by manual, email-based IT requests. By leveraging the MERN stack through internal student developers, this project delivers a customized, zero-budget solution. Through stringent agile sprints, rigorous peer reviews, and comprehensive user testing, the system will provide structured ticket tracking, automated communications, and crucial "Job Card" time management for technicians.
